Semantics, Entities and Superposition: Centroid Tracking for GEO

Researched by Justin Ray October 19, 2026 9 min read TNL Field Note #25

Executive Summary: AI systems convert textual documents into dense high-dimensional vectors. A centroid is the geometric mean of an embedding cluster, generating a single representative anchor for an entire publication catalog, a knowledge domain, or an LLM answer set. Documentation from the Google search leak revealed explicit vector clustering metrics, including siteFocusScore (topical concentration) and siteRadius (deviation from the primary site centroid). In Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), AI citation models select sources through latent proximity distributions rather than static rank slots. By measuring your catalog centroid month over month, you can mathematically prove whether new writing converges on your core knowledge graph entities or dilutes your topical authority.

Latent Space
The learned high-dimensional coordinate system where semantic meaning is quantified as vector distance.
Entity
An unambiguous object or standard, such as C2PA or HPS-1.0, tied to a permanent node in a knowledge graph.
Superposition

Engineering Caveat
Internal API metrics indicate diagnostic monitoring rather than linear ranking levers. Treat centroid drift as an architectural telemetry signal.

// 01. The Foundations: Latent Space, Semantics & Superposition

[Q] What is latent space?

Latent space is the internal geometric coordinate system learned by neural language models. Every sentence, audio file, or technical specification maps to an embedding vector: an ordered list of floating-point numbers. Closeness within that vector space directly tracks closeness in semantic meaning. While the paradigm originated in early word embeddings such as word2vec, modern retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and generative answer engines apply the same mathematical principle to multi-page documents and audio stems. A user query is embedded into a vector, and candidate passages residing within a tight cosine neighborhood are retrieved into context.

[Q] What do "semantics" and "entities" mean in this geometry?

Semantics represents meaning as spatial proximity. Two sentences constructed with disparate vocabulary can sit immediately adjacent in latent space if their contextual meaning aligns. Conversely, two identical phrases used across disparate domains sit distant from one another.

Entities serve as the stable anchors of this geometry. They represent distinct, persistent concepts: people, open standards, cryptographic specifications, or production suites. In computational linguistics, entity linking maps textual tokens to an authoritative node in a knowledge graph. For example, when discussing "Apple," an AI model must determine whether the token represents the botanical fruit, the technology conglomerate, or the record company. In web architecture, Schema.org sameAs triples, Wikidata identifiers, and explicit entity co-occurrences resolve that ambiguity before retrieval occurs.

[Q] What is superposition and polysemanticity?

Research from Anthropic and Harvard demonstrates that neural networks frequently represent significantly more features than their physical architecture has dimensions. They accomplish this through superposition: packing sparse, uncorrelated features into overlapping, almost orthogonal directions while accepting a controlled degree of interference. Because individual neurons simultaneously respond to multiple unrelated concepts, they exhibit polysemanticity.

For technical publishers, superposition provides a vital architectural model. An unanchored term exists in a superposition of ambiguous meanings. When surrounded by tight entity qualifiers, explicit RDF triples, and authoritative co-occurrences, the contextual state collapses into a single, unambiguous coordinate. Ambiguous content renders a blurry, high-variance latent distribution, whereas rigorous entity documentation establishes a razor-sharp centroid.

// 02. Computing Centroids & Topical Radius

[Q] How do you compute a site or catalog centroid?

To establish a reproducible benchmark, embed every document in your archive using a single, static model checkpoint. Normalize each resulting embedding vector to unit length, then calculate their arithmetic mean:

v_i = embed(page_i) / ||embed(page_i)|| c = (1/n) * sum(v_i) r = (1/n) * sum(1 - cos(v_i, c)) # radius: spread around centroid c gap = 1 - cos(c, q) # distance to target query vector q

The metric r measures the topical dispersion of your archive, mirroring the leaked Google search architecture parameters. In those leaked system schemas, siteFocusScore evaluates the topical coherence of an entire domain, while siteRadius quantifies how far peripheral pages deviate from the primary cluster. While we cannot infer their exact weighting within production search stacks, these formulas provide a deterministic framework for monitoring technical authority.

[Q] Why do centroids dictate generative citations and search visibility?

  • Algorithmic Retrieval: When an engine calculates topical authority, a compact document cluster concentrated near the user query vector minimizes cosine distance, qualifying the domain for initial candidate retrieval.
  • Generative Distributions: LLM citations are probabilistic rather than deterministic. Across multi-engine GEO benchmarks, model responses behave like statistical distributions influenced by temperature, geographic context, and prompt phrasing. Maintaining a centroid near the target knowledge cluster maximizes citation probability across varied sampling runs.
  • Content Structure & Verification: Academic research on Generative Engine Optimization indicates that pure keyword repetition produces near-zero visibility lift. In contrast, embedding verifiable statistics, technical formulas, and explicit source attributions delivers the highest measurable increase in citation rates.

// 04. Longitudinal Tracking & The Laws of Semantic Change

[Q] What is the mathematical protocol for tracking centroid trajectory?

  1. Fix the Embedding Checkpoint: Select a standardized embedding model and lock the checkpoint across all evaluation periods. Embedding geometries cannot be directly compared across differing model architectures without complex Procrustes alignment.
  2. Establish Monthly Snapshots: Record monthly vectors for all published assets, calculating the archive centroid c_t, radius r_t, and target query gap gap_t.
  3. Compute Trajectory Velocity: Determine the displacement vector d_t = c_t - c_(t-1) and its Euclidean norm, representing the monthly velocity of topical evolution.
  4. Dimensionality Reduction: Project high-dimensional vectors to 2D using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for visual inspection, while preserving full vector dimensions for mathematical validation.
  5. Benchmark Entity Targets: Overlay the centroids of target search queries, canonical knowledge graph entities, and competitor citations to measure directional convergence.

[Q] What does computational linguistics reveal about semantic movement?

In foundational research on diachronic word embeddings across multi-century corpora (Stanford HistWords), researchers identified two fundamental mathematical principles governing semantic drift:

  • The Law of Conformity: The rate of semantic drift is inversely proportional to word frequency. Concepts that are heavily referenced and rigorously anchored undergo significantly slower semantic change.
  • The Law of Innovation: Highly polysemous words shift meaning at a substantially higher rate, even when controlling for usage frequency.

These findings translate directly to Generative Engine Optimization. Ambiguous, buzzword-heavy content drifts rapidly across latent space, degrading search confidence. Conversely, precise technical documentation anchored by unambiguous RDF triples and verifiable definitions maintains strict geometric stability.

// 05. Engineering Hypotheses: Optimizing Technical Catalogs

Based on latent clustering dynamics, the following five architectural practices provide actionable hypotheses for technical publishers and research hubs:

  1. Citation Gap Vectors: Calculate g = c_cited - c_site, where c_cited is the centroid of pages currently cited by generative answer engines for your target query. The vector g reveals exactly which semantic dimensions your catalog currently lacks. Drafting documentation along this vector systematically shrinks |g|.
  2. Topical Momentum: A platform whose directional cosine alignment remains above 0.70 across consecutive quarters is systematically converging on a subject matter domain. High variance in heading alignment signals fragmented editorial priorities.
  3. Entity Collapse Scoring: For primary technical standards, embed terms in their surrounding context to verify proximity to the intended entity node versus competing colloquial definitions. Adding explicit sameAs schema links expands the margin between correct and conflicting interpretations.
  4. Bridge Documentation: When expanding an archive into an adjacent discipline, publish bridge notes that occupy the intermediate latent space. For example, within the Trust Node Logic archive, documentation linking the Hybrid Production Standard (HPS-1.0), C2PA Cryptographic Provenance, and October 2026 Industry Telemetry prevents catalog fragmentation while expanding coverage.
  5. Probabilistic Mention Sampling: Because generative engines utilize temperature-based decoding, test target queries over 20 independent runs per engine each month. Graphing citation frequency against gap_t proves whether latent distance is your active bottleneck.

// 06. Theoretical & Practical Boundaries

  • Dimensionality Compression: High-dimensional latent geometries (e.g. 1536 or 3072 dimensions) possess topological nuances that are compressed and distorted when rendered in 2D or 3D projections.
  • Model Asymmetry: Your private embedding pipeline (e.g., text-embedding-3-large or open BERT models) differs from proprietary ranking and LLM citation encoders. Local centroid calculations serve as structural proxies rather than identical duplicates of proprietary models.
  • Toy Model Extrapolation: Mechanistic interpretability findings regarding superposition originate primarily in isolated transformer circuits; complex commercial architectures may incorporate additional regularization layers.
  • Non-Linear Ranking Logic: Semantic proximity is a prerequisite for retrieval, but does not override link authority, domain history, click telemetry, or statutory authenticity verification.

// 07. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a centroid in AI latent space?

A centroid is the arithmetic mean of a set of high-dimensional embedding vectors. For a technical website or research archive, it represents the average coordinate of all published pages, summarizing the domain's holistic topical focus into a single point.

What is superposition in a neural network?

Superposition is a computational phenomenon where a neural network represents more features than it has physical dimensions by encoding them into nearly orthogonal, overlapping directions, accepting a minor degree of interference when features are sparse.

Why track centroid movement over time for GEO?

Longitudinal tracking reveals whether new publications move your archive toward or away from the specific entities and queries for which you seek AI citations, protecting against topic dilution and optimizing candidate retrieval probability.
